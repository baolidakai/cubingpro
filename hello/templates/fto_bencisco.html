Face-Turning Octahedron (FTO) Tutorial: Bencisco Method

The Bencisco Method is the premier speedsolving method for the Face-Turning Octahedron (FTO). Developed by Ben Streeter between 2018 and 2019, it revolutionized FTO speedsolving by introducing an efficient block-building framework with superb ergonomics and low move counts.

The name is a portmanteau of Ben's first name (Ben) and Triangular Francisco (a 3x3 method family whose naming scheme was adapted for FTO).

Method Overview

Bencisco structures an FTO solve into four distinct steps:

  1. First Block (dBL Block): Build a 4-piece block on the down-back-left side of the puzzle.
  2. Last 3 Large Centers (L3C): Solve the remaining 3 large center faces while preserving the first block.
  3. Top Corner Pair (UBL Corner & Centers): Solve the UBL corner and its 2 adjacent small centers to finalize the left portion of the puzzle.
  4. Last 3 Triplets (L3T): Solve the remaining corners and centers on the right and front faces using intuitive insertions and algorithmic cases.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

Step 1: First Block (dBL Block)

In this initial phase, you construct a block around the dBL (down-back-left) face. This involves pairing up one large center with its adjacent corner and edge pieces to create a solid anchor block.

Key tip: Because this step is entirely intuitive, practice inspecting the full block during your pre-solve inspection time to achieve a seamless transition into step 2.

Step 2: Last 3 Large Centers (L3C)

With the dBL block stored safely out of the way on your left, you solve the remaining 3 large centers. This step relies on intuitive slice-turn-slice movements (similar to big cube center solving in methods like Yau), ensuring that the first block is never disturbed.

Step 3: UBL Corner and Centers (Top Corner Pair)

Next, you pair up the UBL corner piece along with its two corresponding center pieces. Inserting this piece-group finishes off the entire left/back section of the FTO, leaving only the right/front layer triplets to solve.

Step 4: Last 3 Triplets (L3T)

The final stage solves the remaining 3 sets of corners and small centers (the Last 3 Triplets). Solvers generally accomplish this using a combination of intuitive insertions and algorithmic set combinations (such as 3-cycle commutators and orientation algorithms).

Why Learn Bencisco?

Prior to Bencisco, FTO solving heavily relied on basic reduction methods that suffered from high move counts and awkward rotations. Bencisco offers several major advantages:

  • Great Ergonomics: Keeps the solved block on the left/back, allowing you to execute most moves using fast R, U, and F turns.
  • Low Move Count: A typical Bencisco solve requires significantly fewer moves than standard reduction methods.
  • High TPS & Lookahead: Once the first block is placed, lookahead becomes very fluid through the center and triplet stages.

Improvement & Progression Plan

If you are getting started with FTO speedsolving, here is a recommended path for mastering Bencisco:

  1. Master Block Building: Spend time building the dBL block untimed until you can consistently construct it in under 10-12 moves.
  2. Fluid Center Solving: Practice solving the last 3 large centers without looking at the first block, training yourself to use wide slice turns effortlessly.
  3. Intuitive L3T: Learn the basic 3-cycle commutators for the last 3 triplets before memorizing dedicated algorithm sets.
  4. Full Inspection: Work toward planning the entire dBL block during 15-second inspection.
